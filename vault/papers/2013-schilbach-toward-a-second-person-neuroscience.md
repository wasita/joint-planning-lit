# Toward a second-person neuroscience

*Schilbach, L., Timmermans, B., Reddy, V., Costall, A., Bente, G., Schlicht, T., &amp; Vogeley, K. (2013).* Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 36(4), 393–414.

## Why it's here

The argument that studying social cognition in detached observers misses what is distinctive about being <em>in</em> an interaction — and the target article that licensed a decade of dual-brain work. Directly relevant to any paradigm choosing between third-party judgment and first-person engagement. The open peer commentary is where the objections live.
