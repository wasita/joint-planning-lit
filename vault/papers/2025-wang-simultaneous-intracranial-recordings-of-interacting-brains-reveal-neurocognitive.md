# Simultaneous intracranial recordings of interacting brains reveal neurocognitive dynamics of human cooperation

*Wang, J., Meng, F., Xu, C., Zhang, Y., Liang, K., Han, C., Gao, Y., Yu, X., Li, Z., Zeng, X., Ni, J., Tan, H., Yang, J., &amp; Ma, Y. (2025).* Nature Neuroscience, 28, 161–173.

[journal](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-024-01824-y)

## Why it's here

As far as can be determined, the only published <em>dual-brain simultaneous intracranial</em> study of a cooperation game. High-gamma activity in TPJ and amygdala separately encodes coordinating-with-a-teammate versus pursuing-a-goal, with distinct signatures for establishing versus maintaining cooperation, and cross-brain synchrony predicting coordination success. The coordination/goal-pursuit dissociation maps onto joint-planning theory unusually directly.
