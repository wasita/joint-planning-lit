# Young children's understanding of joint commitments

*Gräfenhain, M., Behne, T., Carpenter, M., &amp; Tomasello, M. (2009).* Developmental Psychology, 45(5), 1430–1443.

[PDF](https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/AmericanPsychologicalAss/Gr%C3%A4fenhain_Young_DevPsych_2009_1553092.pdf)

## Why it's here

The best single empirical entry on commitment specifically rather than cooperation generally, because it manipulates commitment as an independent variable. Children play a game they either explicitly agreed to or that an adult joined uninvited; when the adult then breaks off, three-year-olds — but not two-year-olds — react differentially to the rupture of an agreed commitment, and acknowledge their own departure more often when leaving a mutually agreed activity. Evidence that commitment carries normative force, and a developmental constraint on what the construct has to be.
